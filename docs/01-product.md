# 1. Product

## What owlrnd is

owlrnd is a web app where a school student builds exam confidence by practising short, paper-style exams written for them. A parent sets up what the child practises. Every exam is unique: it stays within the topics the parent set up, and it is shaped by what the student got right and wrong before.

The name plays on "all-rounder": steady practice across everything a child studies, in place of cramming.

## Who it is for

**Parents** are the customer. They sign up, add one or more children, and decide what each child practises. They want to see effort and weak spots at a glance without writing question papers themselves.

**Students** of school age are the daily user. They sign in with a username and password their parent set. They want to know what to do today, finish it in a few minutes, and see that they are improving.

## Core ideas

- **Track.** A series of exams on one chapter or one upcoming test, for example "Physics: Current electricity". A student can have several tracks. There are no "subjects" in 0.1; the subject is part of the track's name.
- **Goal.** Each track has a goal agreed with the parent, such as "9 / 10 or better in 3 exams in a row". When the goal is reached the track stops until the parent adds topics.
- **Exam.** Up to 10 questions, shown together like a printed paper. Multiple choice, short answers and typed answers of a few sentences.
- **Result.** Score, strong and weak topics, and a marked paper with a reason for each miss. The next exam in the track gives more weight to weak topics and repeats some strong ones.
- **Points and levels.** Students earn points for effort and improvement. Points only add up, and the total reaches named levels.

## Principles

- **Adapt between exams, not inside one.** The whole paper is shown at once; the full result decides the next exam.
- **An exam should feel like a real paper.** White page, serif type, numbered questions, one Submit button. The rest of the app is modern and plain.
- **Short and finishable.** Ten questions at most, sized to fit the browser window.
- **Simple for the child.** The dashboard opens on what to do next. History and charts sit on other tabs.
- **Repetition is deliberate.** Weak topics come back until they are strong; strong topics return now and then.
- **The parent is in control.** Parents set up tracks, agree goals and pause tracks. Students practise.
- **One way in.** Parents and students use the same sign-in, and every sign-in is confirmed with a code sent to the parent.
